## What the paper establishes

FRMs denoise Gaussian-to-one-hot interpolants into categorical solutions,
conditioning each update on the previous clean prediction. Fixed-Point Forcing
(FPF) changes training: sample supervision time `t`, sample `t_start ~ U(0,t)`,
roll out the self-conditioned integrator from `t_start` to `t`, and detach its
prediction. Supply that carry to a supervised update at the original
target-derived interpolant. The endpoint cross-entropy stays unchanged;
gradients never traverse the rollout. Table 3 specifies rollout depth 16 and
probability 0.5, following initial self-conditioning training. These are extra
training forward passes. [Sections 2–3, Appendix A.2, Table 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29150v3#page=3)

The reported 44× advantage matches EqR's 98.7% Sudoku-Extreme solve rate using
inference FLOPs. It is not a wall-clock measurement: accounting excludes memory
traffic, framework/kernel overhead and data movement. Evaluations cover Sudoku,
Zebra and mazes, without CFD evidence. Convergence predicts correctness
empirically but provides no correctness certificate. Solved-only first-success
NFE is an oracle diagnostic, not generally deployable stopping.
[Sections 4.1/4.3, Appendix A.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29150v3#page=5)

The authors' linked repository currently provides a README and assets, with the
implementation announced as forthcoming.

## Transfer hypothesis for this project

The potentially useful idea is training on states generated by the model's own
repeated corrections. For a pressure solver, a suitable experiment would collect
detached states after different numbers of corrections, recompute their actual
physical residuals, and train another correction against the pressure/operator
objective. Sampling different correction depths would be our adaptation, not the
paper's sampled flow-time algorithm. This requires no categorical representation
or generative-flow replacement.

Compare that training-only experiment against the existing differentiable
unrolled training under matched data and compute. The relevant question is
whether exposure to later, smaller residuals reduces the passes required to
satisfy the same physical residual gate. More recurrent inference calls cost
more time; savings require fewer calls, cheaper calls, or less fallback work.
Retain the physical residual gate rather than treating agreement between
successive predictions as proof of correctness. These are proposed CFD
adaptations and evaluation criteria, not results established by the paper.

## Codebase comparison and recommendation

This assessment concerns the current C53 pressure-correction family and its
follow-on experiments, rather than the historical fixed-box spectral model in
the root README. The research inference loop already recomputes the remaining
equation residual and applies the same network repeatedly. In schematic form,
`p[k+1] = p[k] + B_theta(b - A p[k])`.
[Inference implementation](../../../experiments/pressure-pass-sweep-v1/inference.py)

Training already includes model-generated intermediate states: the two/four-pass
fine-tuning objective differentiates through shared corrections, supervises the
final physical equation error, adds earlier-pass feedback, and optionally uses a
frozen eight-pass C53 teacher. Detached, variable-depth states from the current
weights would therefore be a specific additional comparison, not the introduction
of recurrent training. Current-policy examples are also already represented in
the captured training inputs.
[Fine-tuning objective](../../../experiments/pressure-four-pass-distillation-v1/fine_tuning.py),
[captured method](../pressure-low-mode-tail-v1/method.md)

The retained four-pass study measured the following on 128 consumed development
requests. The equation target is relative residual <=1e-6. Timings include CPU
recurrence and final equation measurement, excluding simulator/IPC costs.

| C53 policy | Requests meeting equation target | Median time |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Four corrections | 7/128 | 12.20 ms |
| Eight corrections | 128/128 | 24.22 ms |

None of C57–C59 established a qualified reduction to four passes; the later
matched C60/C61 low-mode study also retained C53. Thus reaching eight-pass quality
in four calls could roughly halve this measured inference component, provided
per-call cost stays similar. This is a prospective target, not an observed speedup
or an estimate of complete simulation acceleration.

There is evidence for a different limiting factor: C53's retained 32-case training
diagnosis attributes about 99.8% of remaining four-pass pressure-error energy to
low spatial modes. That measurement concerns pressure error, not equation-error
energy. It supports investigating contraction of large-scale pressure structure;
it does not establish training/inference exposure mismatch as the cause.

Finish the existing C62 fundamental-mode fitting diagnostic first. Its recorded
plan distinguishes inability to fit a single difficult mode from inadequate
coverage, with a conditional frequency curriculum afterward. If a subsequent
experiment tests this paper's transferable idea, keep C53 initialization, data,
precision, physical gates and evaluation populations fixed; compare existing
unrolled training with additional detached, current-weight rollout states. Report
both optimizer updates and actual training work, since rollout forwards cost
compute. Preserve low-mode/early-pass/eight-pass regression guards and require a
qualified four-pass result before independent rollout timing. Use new training
states, not consumed validation targets. No model training or promotion is
authorized or performed by this assessment.
