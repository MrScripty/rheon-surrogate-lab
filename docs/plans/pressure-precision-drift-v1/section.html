Controlled numerical experiment · no neural network

Float32 versus float64 storage drift

These runs use the same Rheon algorithm, pressure solver, tolerances, forces and initial state. Only velocity and tracer storage changes between float32 and float64. Pressure and intermediate arithmetic remain float64 in both. The trajectories separate from step one, without a shared warm-up. This isolates storage-precision effects from training error and differences between solvers.

Accumulated field difference

Independent float32 and float64 trajectories. A repeated float64 run is the null control: its field difference is exactly zero at every recorded step.

Local storage rounding · identical inputs

Each stage is replayed on the same inputs. These curves measure the actual difference between its float64 result and the stored float32 value. Projection also uses identical pressure. They exclude accumulated trajectory differences.

Projection divergence · before and after storage rounding (s⁻¹)

The third curve directly measures divergence of the casting perturbation, D(q32 − q64). It is calculated from the velocity fields, not by subtracting the two maximum-divergence values.

Method, qualification and limits

Four simulations retain every step for 512 steps (8.53 seconds each), including a rectangular domain and a zero-force control. All voxels are cubic. At every step the instrumented float32 path matches Rheon’s original pressure, velocity and tracer buffers bit for bit, and the two float64 runs match each other exactly. Every measured local cast is checked against the original float32 result.

Float64 is a comparison, not exact real arithmetic. Accumulated differences include subsequent responses to rounding, including possible changes in pressure-iteration paths under the same stopping policy. These curves are not a universal float32 accuracy floor and cannot be subtracted from scalar AI drift to determine a training-error percentage. This is not a comparison of float32 versus float64 neural-network inference.
